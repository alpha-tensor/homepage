### One fact, every format the form asks for

Below, one family's paperwork becomes one section of a government form. Four source documents arrive: a Mexican birth certificate, the form that names the case's attorney, a government receipt notice, and a driver license. Watch the sequence, or drive it with the controls: each document's facts land in the part of the form they belong to, in the format that part demands. A name in full here, a middle initial there. An A-number written out, or letter by letter in separate boxes. Hover or tap any value in the form to see the document it came from.

### What you are looking at

Everything on this page is a sample. Gerard Stephen Ashford and Laura María Fernanda Itandehui are invented people, and every document above is a generated sample, labeled as one. No real client's information appears here.

What the sample cannot show is the step that comes first. In production, before any form is filled, the system works out which forms and which evidence a person's situation actually requires, under rules the firm sets and approves. Only then does it collect the facts, once, from the person's own documents, and prepare the packet.

Every value in a finished packet traces back to the document it came from. When two documents disagree, the system flags the conflict for a person to resolve. It never guesses, and it never overwrites. And the packet ends with a person, not a submission: an attorney reviews it and decides the filing.
