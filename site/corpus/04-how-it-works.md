# How it works

## Documents in. Determination first. A packet ready for review out.

### Step 1. The firm sets the rules

Every firm practices differently. Which cases it takes, which packet each case needs, how it handles risk: those are the firm's decisions.

We configure Alpha Tensor to the firm's rules, and the firm approves them. Nothing about the firm's judgment is outsourced. It is written down, applied consistently, and open to inspection.

### Step 2. Documents and answers come in

Every route leads into the same fact base. The client's documents. A completed form from an existing case, or an export from another system, imported whole. And, for what is left, a short intake the firm sends the client as a link.

The order matters. The forms behind a packet carry hundreds of questions, and the documents answer most of them before anyone is asked anything: names, dates, numbers, and addresses, read from IDs, birth certificates, and prior filings. A completed G-28 counts the same way: feed one in and its facts move into every form that needs them.

What remains is the questions no document can answer, the facts only the person knows. Those, and only those, are what the intake asks, in plain language, each one asked once and used everywhere it belongs. The client never faces a stack of blank government forms, and never re-answers a question their own documents already settled.

### Step 3. We determine the packet

Alpha Tensor works out which packet the case needs, under the firm's approved rules. It shows why: which facts drove the decision, and what is missing.

If something does not fit the rules, it is flagged for a person. It is not forced through.

### Step 4. We prepare the packet

We complete the forms the packet requires from the client's documents and answers, and we organize the evidence for review.

Staff see the populated forms they already know, with the source evidence behind each answer. Exceptions are highlighted, not buried.

### Step 5. The attorney reviews

The packet goes to the firm's attorney in a review-ready state. The attorney checks it, changes what needs changing, and makes the filing decision.

That review is the product boundary. We end at review-ready. The firm ends the case.

### One fact, every format it is asked for

Preparation is where other systems hand the work back. Type a fact in once, they say, and then their forms still ask for it three different ways, and your staff type each variation by hand: the full name here, a middle initial there, the address whole on one form and split into street, city, state, and ZIP on the next, an A-number typed into separate boxes one character at a time.

We hold each fact once and render it the way each form requires, automatically. It is unglamorous work, and it is most of what makes a packet right instead of merely filled in.

### Why the order matters

Since August 2026, USCIS can deny a filing for missing evidence without asking for it first. A missing document can now cost the case, not just time.

That is why determination comes before preparation, and preparation comes before anything goes out. Getting the packet right up front is no longer about speed. It is about not losing the case, the fee, and the place in line.
