# Alpha Tensor Homepage Motif Direction

## Status

Planning and implementation specification.

## Purpose

Refine the Alpha Tensor homepage visual system so the grid and dot
matrix stop behaving like persistent background wallpaper and become a
deliberate visual language for transformation, structure, and system
activity.

The homepage should retain its warm editorial foundation, restrained
orange brand language, technical precision, and existing product UI. The
primary change is to introduce a concentrated colored matrix motif in
the hero and establish rules for how that motif evolves throughout the
page.

The goal is not to make Alpha Tensor look more futuristic. The goal is
to make the visual system more recognizable, purposeful, and connected
to the product thesis.

Alpha Tensor takes unstructured information, identifies useful facts,
imposes structure, and turns those facts into actionable workflows. The
page motif should quietly demonstrate that same transformation.

---

## 1. Core Direction

Move away from:

- A uniform beige grid covering most light sections.
- Identical dot density throughout the page.
- Grid, dots, lines, and orange accents acting as unrelated
  decoration.
- Predictable graph-paper backgrounds.
- Generic AI gradients.
- Persistent visual texture that competes with product content.

Move toward:

- Large areas of visual quiet.
- Localized grid and matrix fields.
- A concentrated colored matrix field in the hero.
- Dots, lines, coordinates, and grids as states of one system.
- Motif density that changes according to information density.
- Content surfaces that interrupt and occlude the motif.
- A visual progression from unstructured to structured information.
- Warm technical instrumentation rather than cyberpunk styling.

---

## 2. Visual Thesis

The motif should communicate:

> Unstructured information enters. Alpha Tensor detects meaningful
> structure. Structured information becomes usable work.

The motif therefore has multiple states.

### State A. Ambient Signal

Sparse dots with little or no visible grid.

Represents incoming information and latent structure.

### State B. Detection

Localized clusters become more visible.

Some dots gain color or increased intensity.

Represents the system identifying meaningful regions.

### State C. Alignment

Partial horizontal and vertical guides appear.

Dots begin aligning around product artifacts.

Represents extraction and normalization.

### State D. Structure

A more regular grid appears around structured records, forms, case data,
or workflow UI.

Represents information becoming operational.

### State E. Resolution

The structure becomes sparse again.

The page returns to clean editorial space.

Represents the system delivering useful output without exposing
unnecessary complexity.

These states should appear throughout the page rather than using one
continuous background pattern.

---

## 3. Hero Direction

The hero should become the strongest expression of the motif.

### Recommended Composition

Use a split composition.

Left side:

- Headline.
- Supporting copy.
- Primary CTA.
- Minimal motif interference.
- Warm editorial surface or controlled dark transition depending on
  final hero treatment.

Right side:

- Product demonstration.
- Floating document artifacts.
- Structured UI.
- Concentrated colored matrix field.
- Highest visual activity in the hero.

The matrix should originate primarily around the product demonstration
and dissolve before reaching the primary headline.

Do not place an equally strong matrix behind both sides.

### Hero Coverage

Target approximately:

- 40 to 55 percent of hero width containing visible matrix activity.
- 20 to 35 percent containing high density matrix activity.
- Remaining area visually quiet.

The exact percentages can vary responsively.

### Preferred Hero Treatment

Test the dark hero first.

Use a near-black or ink substrate behind the matrix and product
composition.

The existing cream page should begin immediately after the hero or after
a short transition.

This creates a stronger opening and gives the colored matrix enough
contrast to remain subtle rather than requiring excessive saturation.

A light hero remains a fallback if the dark treatment harms readability
or continuity.

---

## 4. Color System

The reference image should inform behavior, not palette.

Avoid a rainbow or RGB spectrum.

### Primary Ambient Colors

Use colors derived from the existing Alpha Tensor identity.

Primary:

- Burnt orange.
- Ember.
- Warm red-orange.
- Deep oxblood.

Secondary, used sparingly:

- Muted violet.
- Dusty plum.
- Very restrained cool blue.

Neutral:

- Ink.
- Warm black.
- Cream.
- Warm gray.

### Color Hierarchy

Solid brand orange:

Reserved for interaction, emphasis, CTA elements, selected states, and
intentional rules.

Luminous orange:

Used inside the matrix as ambient activity.

Muted violet and blue:

Used to create depth and spatial variation, not as primary brand colors.

### Important Rule

Do not use a conventional visible gradient as the motif.

The perceived gradient should emerge from variation in individual dot
color and intensity.

The substrate should remain visible between dots.

---

## 5. Matrix Construction

The matrix is the primary primitive.

It should be capable of becoming:

- Sparse signal dots.
- Dense matrix fields.
- Alignment points.
- Partial grid intersections.
- Coordinate markers.
- Processing trails.
- Section transition artifacts.

### Dot Behavior

Dots should not have identical intensity.

Introduce controlled variation in:

- Opacity.
- Color.
- Local density.
- Radius.
- Activation.
- Clustering.

Variation must remain subtle.

Do not introduce obvious random noise.

The result should look designed, not procedurally chaotic.

### Dot Size

Use a small base size.

Recommended starting range:

- Desktop diameter, 1 to 2 pixels.
- High-density display diameter, visually equivalent to approximately
  1 CSS pixel.
- Mobile diameter, 1 to 1.5 pixels.

Avoid large LED-like dots.

### Dot Spacing

Recommended initial range:

- Dense field, 7 to 10 pixels.
- Standard field, 10 to 14 pixels.
- Sparse field, 16 to 28 pixels.

Spacing can transition within a composition, but avoid continuously
scaling the entire matrix.

---

## 6. Grid System

The grid should no longer be a global background.

### Base Rule

The grid is structural decoration, not page wallpaper.

### Coverage

Across light sections:

- No more than approximately 30 to 40 percent of the visible area
  should contain clearly perceptible grid structure.
- Major sections should include substantial clean regions.
- Consecutive sections should not use identical grid fields.

### Grid Fields

Grid fields should attach to:

- Product UI.
- Document previews.
- Metrics.
- Diagrams.
- Workflow illustrations.
- Section transitions.
- Extraction examples.

Fields should have boundaries.

Boundaries may:

- Fade.
- Terminate.
- Be occluded.
- Break into dots.
- Lose subdivisions.
- Disappear behind content.

### Grid Hierarchy

Support at least three levels.

Macro grid:

Very faint structural spacing.

Local grid:

Visible only around active compositions.

Micro matrix:

Dots or fine intersections representing active information.

Do not render all three levels everywhere.

---

## 7. Occlusion

Occlusion should become an intentional part of the identity.

Product cards, documents, screenshots, text blocks, and UI surfaces
should interrupt the motif.

Do not allow the grid to remain visibly continuous underneath every
object.

Use masks to create:

- Missing grid cells.
- Erased rectangles.
- Broken lines.
- Partially hidden matrix fields.
- Abrupt local interruptions.

This should suggest that the product interface exists above the
underlying system rather than being printed onto graph paper.

---

## 8. Partial Coordinate Language

Introduce occasional technical annotations without turning the site into
a schematic.

Possible primitives:

- Short horizontal guide.
- Short vertical guide.
- Single orange intersection.
- Small coordinate tick.
- Tiny index number.
- Partial bounding corner.
- Registration mark.
- Small matrix cluster.
- Incomplete measurement line.

These elements should usually be decorative and should not imply false
quantitative meaning.

Avoid dense pseudo-engineering labels.

---

## 9. Section Progression

The motif should change as the visitor moves through the page.

### Navigation

Very restrained.

The navigation should remain clean.

At most, allow a trace of the hero matrix to exist behind or below it.

### Hero

Highest atmospheric color.

Dark substrate preferred.

Colored matrix concentrated around product demonstration.

Headline area remains relatively quiet.

### 1,100 Plus Proof Section

Strong interruption.

Keep this section typographically dominant.

Use very sparse matrix or coordinate fragments.

Do not compete with the metric.

### Product Explanation

Return to cream.

Use localized neutral grids.

Matrix loses most hero color.

Product cards begin aligning to visible structural guides.

### Intake and Processing Explanation

Increase technical structure.

Use dots transitioning into lines and partial grids.

This is the most appropriate area for the visual metaphor of information
becoming structured.

### Case Management Section

Use a more regular grid around structured case UI.

The motif should feel resolved and orderly.

Avoid high atmospheric color.

### Capabilities Section

Return to mostly clean space.

Use small independent matrix fragments to identify capability groups.

### Integration or System Section

Grid can become somewhat denser again.

Use alignment and connection motifs.

Avoid literal network diagrams unless they convey actual product
behavior.

### Dark Performance Section

Use sparse illuminated dots.

Orange and muted violet can return.

Do not repeat the hero at full intensity.

The hero should remain the strongest colored matrix moment.

### Final CTA

Reduce almost everything.

Use mostly clean cream or dark space.

One small motif fragment can remain as a signature.

The visitor should end with clarity rather than visual complexity.

The page should alternate between activity and rest.

Recommended rhythm:

Quiet.

Structure.

Quiet.

Dense system activity.

Resolution.

Quiet CTA.

Avoid:

Grid.

## 11. Relationship to Product UI

The motif must support actual product artifacts.

It should never become more visually important than:

- Uploaded documents.
- Extracted fields.
- Case information.
- Forms.
- Workflow states.
- Product screenshots.
- Demonstrated outcomes.

When product UI is present, the motif should behave as context.

When no product UI is present, the motif may carry more compositional
responsibility.

---

## 12. Motion

Motion should be extremely restrained.

Do not create:

- Constantly moving particles.
- Pulsing RGB effects.
- Star fields.
- Scanning lasers.
- Large animated gradients.
- Matrix rain.
- Decorative cursor tracking.

Acceptable motion includes:

- Slow opacity drift within a small subset of dots.
- A few dots activating when product artifacts enter.
- Short line segments resolving into alignment.
- Local matrix intensity increasing during a product transition.
- Very slow background color movement that is perceptible only over
  several seconds.

Motion should suggest computation without demanding attention.

### Reduced Motion

Respect prefers-reduced-motion.

The static composition must remain complete without animation.

---

## 13. Interaction

The motif should generally not react to pointer movement.

If interaction is introduced, keep it directly tied to product behavior.

Examples:

- Hovering an extraction field subtly illuminates corresponding
  alignment points.
- Selecting a document causes a nearby matrix cluster to become more
  structured.
- A workflow step activates a short coordinate path.

Do not make the entire background chase the cursor.

---

## 14. Technical Implementation Strategy

Prefer CSS and lightweight SVG where possible.

Avoid large raster background assets for the primary motif.

Recommended layers:

1.  Substrate.
2.  Low-frequency color field.
3.  Dot matrix.
4.  Optional structural grid.
5.  Occlusion masks.
6.  Product artifacts.
7.  Foreground annotations.

### CSS Responsibilities

Use CSS for:

- Substrate colors.
- Repeating dot patterns where appropriate.
- Masks.
- Opacity transitions.
- Section fades.
- Simple ambient fields.

### SVG Responsibilities

Use SVG for:

- Irregular matrix regions.
- Controlled dot activation.
- Partial guides.
- Registration marks.
- Coordinate fragments.
- More intentional color distribution.

### Canvas

Do not introduce Canvas unless profiling demonstrates a clear reason.

This motif does not justify adding an unnecessarily complex rendering
system.

Keep motif behavior composable.

Suggested conceptual components:

```text

CoordinateMark

```

Example conceptual API:

```text
MotifField
  variant = hero | process | structured | sparse | dark
  density = sparse | normal | dense
  color = neutral | ember | mixed
  fade = left | right | top | bottom | radial | custom
  occlusion = true | false
  motion = none | subtle
```

Do not expose dozens of arbitrary visual properties at call sites.

Create a small number of approved variants and tune them centrally.

## 17. Responsive Behavior

Do not simply shrink the desktop motif.

### Desktop

Use full composition.

Allow asymmetrical fields.

Support several motif layers.

### Tablet

Reduce field width and density.

Preserve the relationship between product artifacts and matrix activity.

Remove secondary annotations before shrinking primary ones.

### Mobile

Prioritize content.

Use one concentrated matrix region rather than multiple fields.

Reduce:

- Grid subdivisions.
- Coordinate annotations.
- Ambient color variation.
- Decorative line segments.

The mobile hero can retain the matrix behind or immediately surrounding
the product artifact, but text readability remains dominant.

---

## 18. Accessibility

The motif is decorative.

It must not be required to understand content.

Requirements:

- Maintain text contrast independently of the motif.
- Avoid placing small body text over high-activity matrix regions.
- Decorative SVG should be hidden from assistive technology.
- Motion must not convey unique information.
- Respect reduced motion.
- Avoid rapid luminance changes.
- Avoid patterns that create excessive visual vibration.

## 21. Motif Variation Rules

To prevent the current over-consistency problem, enforce these rules.

1.  Never repeat the same motif variant in adjacent major sections.

2.  At least one major section between dense motif fields should be
    mostly clean.

3.  Do not use identical matrix density across the page.

4.  Do not use identical grid opacity across the page.

5.  Do not center every motif field.

6.  Do not make every field rectangular.

7.  Do not populate every grid intersection with dots.

8.  Do not allow every line to run edge to edge.

9.  Do not use color in every motif field.

10. Do not make every section visually self-contained with the same
    decorative treatment.

11. Content should occlude motifs where appropriate.

12. The hero remains the strongest colored motif.

13. Product structure determines motif placement whenever possible.

14. Decorative randomness must remain bounded by approved variants.

## 23. Implementation Sequence

### Phase 1. Remove Excess

Before adding the new hero treatment:

- Reduce or remove the current global grid.
- Identify every existing grid and dot treatment.
- Remove repeated section-level background patterns.
- Establish clean cream surfaces.
- Preserve only grids that currently explain composition or product
  structure.

This phase matters. Adding the new motif without removing the old
repetition will make the page busier rather than better.

### Phase 2. Build Hero Prototype

Create one isolated hero experiment.

Include:

- Dark substrate.
- Matrix field.
- Restricted color palette.
- Existing headline.
- Existing CTA.
- Existing product demonstration.
- Controlled fade between quiet and active regions.

Do not modify the rest of the page until this composition works.

### Phase 3. Establish Motif Primitives

Extract reusable:

- Matrix field.
- Grid field.
- Mask.
- Coordinate mark.
- Registration mark.
- Fade.

Create approved variants.

### Phase 4. Recompose Light Sections

Apply neutral localized fields to existing cream sections.

Do not add hero-level color.

Use motif only where it reinforces the content.

### Phase 5. Create Transformation Narrative

Review the entire page vertically.

Adjust motif states so the page moves from:

Signal.

Detection.

Structure.

Operational output.

Resolution.

### Phase 6. Add Minimal Motion

Only after static composition is approved.

Motion is enhancement, not foundation.

### Phase 7. Responsive Pass

Tune desktop, tablet, and mobile independently.

### Phase 8. Performance and Accessibility

Verify:

- Paint cost.
- Animation cost.
- Layout stability.
- Reduced motion.
- Contrast.
- Mobile performance.

## 24. Acceptance Criteria

The direction is successful when:

- The grid is no longer perceived as wallpaper.
- The hero has a distinctive Alpha Tensor visual signature.
- The colored matrix does not resemble a generic rainbow AI gradient.
- Orange remains clearly associated with the brand.
- Product UI remains more important than decoration.
- Large clean areas provide visual rest.
- Motif density visibly changes throughout the page.
- The motif supports the concept of information becoming structured.
- Dark sections feel related without simply repeating the hero.
- The page remains recognizable with all animation disabled.
- Mobile remains materially simpler than desktop.
- A screenshot from the hero is visually identifiable without relying
  entirely on the logo.

---

## 25. Failure Conditions

Rework the implementation if:

- The first impression is cyberpunk.
- The first impression is RGB gaming hardware.
- The hero looks like a generic AI startup gradient.
- The matrix is equally bright across its entire area.
- Purple or blue becomes more prominent than orange.
- The motif competes with the headline.
- Every section visibly contains a grid.
- Consecutive sections use the same dot spacing.
- The grid remains visible continuously behind cards.
- Decorative elements imply fake data or fake measurements.
- Animation is necessary for the composition to work.
- Mobile feels like a compressed desktop screenshot.
- Performance requires Canvas or a heavy graphics dependency without
  demonstrated need.

---

## 26. Final Design Principle

The matrix is not decoration added to Alpha Tensor.

It represents latent structure.

The grid represents detected structure.

The product interface represents usable structure.

The homepage should move between those states deliberately.

The strongest visual moments should therefore occur where Alpha Tensor
is actively transforming information, while the rest of the page remains
quiet enough for the product and message to carry the experience.

## 27. Motion

Direction recorded 2026-09-24. Not implemented. Do not tune before the static
surface pass is approved.

### 27.1 Principle

The animation should be almost imperceptible. The reference should feel alive,
not animated.

    Fixed physical matrix
            +
    Slow activity underneath it
            =
    Living system surface

### 27.2 The three approved motions

1. Very slow chromatic drift. The broad violet, plum and ember fields move a few
   percent over roughly 20 to 40 seconds. The dot lattice itself never moves.
   This creates the impression of activity underneath a fixed physical screen.
2. Extremely subtle intensity breathing. One or two chromatic regions vary
   opacity by perhaps 5 to 10 percent over long, asynchronous periods. No
   synchronized pulsing. You should not be able to point at the animation
   immediately.
3. Initial settling on page load. The field begins slightly quieter and reaches
   its normal state over roughly 1 to 2 seconds. Instrumentation coming online,
   not a hero animation. The product UI does not need a dramatic entrance.

### 27.3 Rejected

Moving dots. Waves through the lattice. Scanning lines. Cursor interaction.
Parallax dots. Random twinkling. Gradients moving fast enough to notice.

All of these would undo the work that got the motif away from stars and generic
tech imagery.

### 27.4 The test

If someone immediately says "the background is moving", it is too much. Ideally
they notice after several seconds that the surface is not completely static.

### 27.5 Implementation separation

The dot screen remains completely static. Only the underlying chromatic layers
are animation candidates. The screen, the ink, the masks and the layout are never
animation candidates.

### 27.6 Prototype protocol

Do not tune the animation before the static surface is approved. Then build one
prototype with exaggerated speed, roughly 5 to 8 seconds, purely to verify the
direction. Once the movement is correct, slow it dramatically to the production
duration, 20 to 40 seconds. Verify at the exaggerated speed, ship at the slow
speed.

### 27.7 Requirement on the static implementation, and the candidate architectures

The colour fields must remain independently addressable, so that they can later
receive slow transform and opacity animation without changing the core, the
screen, the masks or the layout.

State as of 2026-09-24: they are not. `--tone-stack` is one custom property
holding seven radial gradients, declared once on `.field` and consumed by both
`.core` and `.atmosphere`. That coupling is deliberate and correct for the static
pass, because it is what guarantees the two layers cannot diverge in colour, and
that is what keeps the cream transition chromatic instead of grey. But it also
means there is no element and no value per region to animate.

Two ways to satisfy the requirement, and a third that may avoid it entirely. All
deferred until after approval.

- Registered numeric custom properties. One `@property` declaration per tone,
  `syntax: "<number>"`, with `--tone-stack` rewritten to consume them instead of
  literal alphas. No DOM change, no mask change, no layout change, and both layers
  stay in lockstep because they still share the string. Cost: a registered
  property feeding `background-image` is not compositor animatable, so the
  animation repaints both layers every frame on the main thread for as long as it
  runs.
- One element per chromatic region, animated with `transform` and `opacity`.
  Compositor only, and genuinely independent per region. Cost: it deletes the
  single shared string that keeps the core and the atmosphere coherent, and it
  breaks three of the existing invariants.
- Broad compositor overlays. Leave the approved static tone stack exactly as it
  is, all seven gradients, the masks, the ink and the screen untouched, and add
  one or two extremely wide, very faint chromatic overlays above or below it, a
  violet and an ember, moving slowly with `transform` and `opacity`. This produces
  perceived chromatic drift while the approved static surface never changes and
  never repaints its gradient stack.

Recommendation, revised. Neither of the first two is the presumed production
architecture. Registered property animation is the first experiment, not the
answer, because repainting a background stack continuously for 20 to 40 seconds
is not obviously worth accepting on a landing page.

The overlay architecture is the leading candidate for production, because it
preserves static fidelity by construction, which is the property that matters
most. It is also not the same thing as replacing seven static fields with seven
elements. It adds to the system rather than rebuilding it.

One caveat to settle before anyone builds it. An overlay leaves the tone stack
untouched, but it cannot leave the rendered image untouched, because it is painted
over the field. Two consequences follow. If the overlay carries a constant low
alpha, the approved static appearance is gone permanently, which defeats the point
of preserving the baseline at all. And a broad overlay will also lift the interior
of the field, where the product surfaces sit, not only the outer transition,
unless it is confined by a mask of its own, and masks are exactly what the
invariants police.

The version that avoids both is an overlay that breathes through zero, so the
approved rendering is a recurring instant rather than a state that has been lost,
and whose geometry is sized and centred so its influence behind the card is close
to nothing. That keeps `motif-invariants` true as written and keeps the frozen
baseline meaningful. Prove it against that baseline rather than by eye.

So the decomposition becomes conditional. Do it render neutrally only if an
experiment actually needs it, and only once the approved static baseline exists to
compare against. The overlays may make it unnecessary entirely.

### 27.8 Cost containment

An unbounded full area repaint for the life of a landing page is a battery bug.
The animation must be gated on hero visibility, so it costs nothing once the
visitor has scrolled past. There is already precedent for this pattern in the
codebase.

Reduced motion is already covered. The blanket rule in `apps/www/src/index.css`
reduces every animation duration to 0.01ms and pins iteration count to 1, so a
CSS only animation needs no extra reduced motion work. Verify it, do not assume
it.

The `.play` gate on `visualColumn` in `Hero.tsx`, applied 150ms after mount, is
the existing hook for motion 3 and needs no new plumbing.

### 27.9 Gate

The `design-paper` drift reading only inspects animations whose name contains
`paper`, so it is blind to this work and cannot be cited as evidence either way.
If the motion should be pinned, it needs its own invariant in the style of
`apps/www/scripts/tests/motif-invariants.test.mjs`: production durations inside
the approved range, no animation declared on the screen layer, and no transform
on the dot pitch.

### 27.10 Sequence

1. Finish and approve the static surface. Freeze its screenshot and the relevant
   measurements as the baseline.
2. Preserve that exact static rendering before introducing any motion.
3. Treat registered custom property animation as the first prototype, not the
   presumed production architecture.
4. Prototype movement at 5 to 8 seconds.
5. Determine the minimum number of independently moving chromatic channels
   needed. Do not assume all seven tones need animation. Two may be enough, one
   cool and one warm, with the remaining gradients static. That would cut both
   complexity and repaint work substantially.
6. Once the movement is right, test the intended 20 to 40 second behaviour and
   measure continuous paint cost on desktop and mobile. Visibility gating is
   mandatory.
7. If continuous repaint is materially expensive, do not compromise the static
   motif in order to preserve animation. Test the overlay architecture in 27.7
   instead.

### 27.11 Never animate

The dot screen. The dot pitch. Individual dots. The core mask. The layout.

No particles, no twinkling, no scanning, no cursor response.

### 27.12 Priority

Static visual fidelity has priority over animation. The target is a matrix that
appears physically fixed while some extremely slow activity seems to exist
underneath it. Use the cheapest architecture that convincingly produces that
illusion.

## 28. Silhouette

Recorded 2026-09-24, after the matrix was made visible. Supersedes the rectangular
field model in sections 6 and 7 for the hero core.

### 28.1 The failure

With the 6px screen at 0.13 alpha the motif became perceptible, and that exposed
the remaining structural problem. The visible silhouette was a rectangle. The top,
the left, the bottom and both bottom corners were all identifiable, and because
the matrix filled that rectangle evenly it reinforced the shape instead of
breaking it up. The result reads as a panel with softened edges rather than as a
chromatic surface.

The higher dot visibility is not the cause and must be kept. It is what made the
shape legible enough to diagnose.

### 28.2 Model

Discard the model of a dark rectangle faded outward. A rectangle with softened
edges is still a rectangle, and more blur only makes a larger blurry rectangle.

    Dark rectangle, then fade the rectangle outward               (rejected)
    Overlapping broad chromatic masses that together leave
    enough dark surface behind the product                        (required)

The field DOM box stays rectangular, because that is implementation detail. The
visible alpha must not resemble those bounds.

### 28.3 Core

Build the core opacity from three overlapping masses rather than one mask with four
softened edges.

| Mass       | Carries                                        |
| :--------- | :--------------------------------------------- |
| Upper      | the floating documents and the top of the card |
| Central    | the main card                                  |
| Lower warm | the queue and the space around it              |

Their overlap creates the substrate. They must not individually read as circles,
which means generous overlap and different sizes.

The visible region must have no identifiable top edge, left edge, bottom edge,
bottom left corner or bottom right corner. Every mass must therefore reach zero
alpha inside the box, because a mask cannot paint outside its element and an opaque
mask at the box boundary prints a straight edge.

Do not use `mask-composite: intersect`. Intersecting a horizontal ramp with a
vertical ramp is precisely what produces the rectangle. The masses union instead,
which is the default, so the hero core mask must contain no `linear-gradient` at
all. That absence is the machine checkable form of this rule.

### 28.4 Composition

The motif should not surround the product equally, and probably should not surround
it at all. The strongest field sits above and behind the right side of the card, and
underneath the queue. The left side disappears much sooner. That protects the copy
and makes the motif feel attached to the product rather than attached to the hero
section.

### 28.5 Atmosphere

Keep the two layer architecture. The atmosphere still carries chroma beyond the core
with no ink and no screen. It becomes organic radial falloff as well, using the same
centres as the core with larger radii, so the relationship that keeps the cream
transition chromatic survives in radial form: through the core's fade the atmosphere
is the more opaque of the two.

It must fall to essentially zero before it reaches meaningful body copy.

### 28.6 Matrix

Pitch stays 6px. The increased visibility is directionally right. Do not increase it
further in this pass and do not return to the barely visible state it replaced. The
screen is a layer inside the core, so it inherits the organic silhouette without any
change of its own. Dots must disappear with the core rather than forming a
rectangular sheet outside it.

If the new silhouette genuinely needs it, screen alpha may come down slightly
afterwards. Do not lead with that.

### 28.7 Colour

Not retuned in this pass. The violet, plum and warm relationship is sufficient to
judge geometry, and gradients must not be added in order to hide edges.

### 28.8 Copy safety

A hard constraint at desktop widths. The body copy region must remain visually
equivalent to plain cream.

- no matrix dots underneath it
- no dark substrate underneath it
- only a very weak chromatic atmosphere may approach it

This is a presence constraint, not a contrast ratio. Dots behind body copy are
objectionable at any contrast, so measuring mean luminance does not catch it. The
measurement has to look for high frequency variation under the copy, not for average
darkening.

### 28.9 Validation

Edge softness alone is not the success metric. Sample the perimeter of the visible
motif and measure where it disappears.

- The left edge X must differ materially across rows. If the left edge X is roughly
  constant over most rows, the rectangle is still visible and the pass fails.
- The bottom must not disappear at a single Y coordinate across the field width.
- The copy region must stay at cream with dot amplitude near zero beneath it.

Judge desktop before touching responsive. The responsive overrides still carry the
rectangular model and are out of scope for this pass.
